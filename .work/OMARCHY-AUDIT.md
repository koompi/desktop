# Audit — basecamp/omarchy vs KOOMPI desktop

Omarchy at `~/.tmp/omarchy`, commit `b86d450` (2026-08-25, `version` = `4.0.0.alpha`). Ours: `main` at `180d2f9d`.
Omarchy paths are relative to that clone; ours to this repo.

Scope set by Rithy: what to take (code, design, functionality, UX, some UI, app choices) and what not to.
Constraints: the top panel bar stays (`dots/.config/quickshell/koompi/modules/koompi/bar`), the identity stays, we are not becoming omarchy.

What is already covered elsewhere and not repeated here:
- `.work/archive/2026-08-24-visual-polish/AUDIT.md` V1-V3: Plymouth, GRUB, installer TUI rendering.
- The omarchy-port campaign merged at `f4834f3f` (J03-J11): argv-safe `koompi-notify-send`, colour fan-out to Konsole/Zed/AI CLIs, timestamped migrations, AI-usage bar widget, cidata/OEM install, `koompi hook|webapp|plugin|snapshot`. - `.work/AUDIT.md` B1-B3 / J11: the packaged update route delivers nothing because the `[koompi]` repo is a skeleton. Rows below that depend on it say so.

Effort: S under a day, M a few days, L a week or more. Rows are ranked by value to a KOOMPI user (school and office laptops, often 4-8 GB, often on battery, often offline).

## NOT TAKING

- Named theme packs as the theming model (`themes/*/colors.toml`, 22 of them, `manual/43-making-your-own-theme.md:3-5`): wallpaper-derived Material You is ours (`docs/agents/theming.md:9-11`); a fixed palette list is omarchy's identity, not a feature we lack.
- The Omarchy menu as its own `Super+Space` surface (`default/hypr/bindings/utilities.lua:1`): Search on `Super` release is our launcher (`docs/navigation.md:12-24`); O05 folds the tree into Search rather than adding a surface.
- Universal `Super+C/V/X` clipboard (`default/hypr/bindings/clipboard.lua:45-47`): collides with `Super+C` editor and `Super+X` text editor (`keybinds.lua:421,423`) and `Super+V` history (`keybinds.lua:112-114`); retraining every user's fingers for a terminal edge case.
- `Ctrl+Alt+Del` closes all windows (`default/hypr/bindings/tiling.lua:3`): ours is the session menu (`keybinds.lua:74`), the reflex our users bring from Windows.
- pacman guard that aborts `pacman -Syu` (`default/libalpm/hooks/00-omarchy-update-guard.hook`, `bin/omarchy-update-pacman-guard:46-60`): we notify pending migrations at login instead (O03), and "none of it is load-bearing" (`README.md:140`) is a promise we keep to Arch users.
- Terminal-hosted screensaver and ASCII branding (`bin/omarchy-screensaver:31-34`, `bin/omarchy-branding-about`): ASCII art is omarchy's look; O32 is a Quickshell layer with the SVG mark.
- Terminal picker across Alacritty/Foot/Ghostty/Kitty (`bin/omarchy-default-terminal`): WezTerm is the decision (`sdata/dist-arch/koompi-apps/PKGBUILD:35-41`), Kitty the fallback.
- Chromium default plus Google-account and yt-dlp host integrations (`bin/omarchy-install-chromium-*`, `install/config/theme-system.sh:9-16`): Chrome then Brave is ours (`dots/.config/hypr/hyprland/variables.lua:13`); no Google host hooks.
- HEY, Basecamp, X, Google web apps preinstalled and bound (`applications/*.desktop`, `default/hypr/bindings/applications.lua:22-33`): DHH's set; ours is WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord widgets (`keybinds.lua:53-55`).
- Gaming installers and the Windows VM (`bin/omarchy-install-gaming-*`, `bin/omarchy-windows-vm`): not a school or office target.
- mise-managed agent installs (`install/user/mise.sh`): `sdata/install/agents.sh` installs agents user-locally from their own channels already; same result, one less tool.
- Docker by default, lazydocker, sudoless-docker wizard (`install/omarchy-base.packages`, `bin/omarchy-setup-security-sudoless-docker`): KOOMPI's container story is The Subsystem (`CONTEXT.md`), not the docker group.
- Code-denylist for installed themes (`bin/omarchy-theme-set:20-31`): our themes never carry code, so there is nothing to deny.
- Neovim as the default editor with a bundled distro (`install/omarchy-base.packages` `omarchy-nvim`): Zed is ours (`README.md:128`).
- Passwordless sudo wizard (`bin/omarchy-sudo-passwordless`): no, and especially not on shared machines.
